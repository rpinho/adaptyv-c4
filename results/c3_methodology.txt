We combined three complementary approaches to design de novo single-chain binders against the GLP1R extracellular domain (ECD), targeting the hydrophobic groove that natively accommodates GLP-1's N-terminal helix.

Approach 1 — BindCraft (9 designs): We used BindCraft with forced targeting on the GLP1R ECD from PDB 6X18 (chain R). Hotspot residues were selected to cover the groove: W33, W39, R40, E68, Y69, W91 and surrounding positions (A67-A69, A88-A91, A121, A123, A128, A137-A145). The campaign explored binder lengths 45-100 residues over 3000 trajectories, yielding 10 ranked designs. One was dropped for excessive steric clashes (13 all-atom clashes). The remaining 9 designs span iPTM 0.76-0.85, AF2 pLDDT 0.83-0.93, with full contact across all 6 key groove residues.

Approach 2 — Point-mutation variants (9 designs): Structural analysis of the top 3 BindCraft designs identified interface-edge positions where charge optimization could improve binding. We introduced E→Q, Q→K, and F→Y substitutions at positions near the binding interface to move net charge closer to neutral while preserving or improving contacts with groove residues. Each variant differs by 1-2 mutations from its parent.

Approach 3 — RFD4-Proteina (2 designs): We used NVIDIA's RFD4-Proteina diffusion model as an orthogonal generation method, targeting the same GLP1R ECD groove with hotspot conditioning on W33, W39, R40, E68, Y69, W91, Y88, and R121. 200 designs were generated, and the top 2 by sequence diversity and charge balance were selected.

All 20 designs were independently validated using ESMFold (facebook/esmfold_v1) single-sequence structure prediction. The BindCraft designs achieved refold pLDDT 68.4-89.6, the point-mutation variants 84.2-89.6, confirming that designed sequences fold into well-defined structures without relying on MSA or template information.
