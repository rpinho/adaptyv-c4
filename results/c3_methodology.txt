We combined three complementary approaches to design de novo single-chain binders against the GLP1R extracellular domain (ECD), targeting the hydrophobic groove that natively accommodates GLP-1's C-terminal helix. These are ECD-groove binders; agonism is not structurally established by this design evidence.

Approach 1 — BindCraft (8 designs): We used BindCraft with forced targeting on the GLP1R ECD from PDB 6X18 (chain R). Hotspot residues were selected to cover the groove: W33, W39, R40, E68, Y69, W91 and surrounding positions (A67-A69, A88-A91, A121, A123, A128, A137-A145). The campaign explored binder lengths 45-100 residues over 3000 trajectories, yielding 10 ranked designs. Two were dropped: one for excessive steric clashes (13 all-atom clashes), one for marginal ESMFold refold confidence (pLDDT 68.4). The remaining 8 designs span iPTM 0.76-0.85, AF2 pLDDT 0.83-0.93, with full contact across all 6 key groove residues.

Approach 2 — Point-mutation variants (7 designs in v6; 1 retained in the final v8 portfolio): Structural analysis of the top 3 BindCraft designs identified interface-edge positions where charge optimization could improve binding. We introduced E→Q, Q→K, and F→Y substitutions at positions near the binding interface to move net charge closer to neutral while preserving or improving contacts with groove residues. Each variant differs by 1-2 mutations from its parent. Variant count was reduced from 9 to 7 for v6. Round-1 AF2M complex screening then showed that six of those seven variants were near-duplicates of retained backbones, so v7 removed six of them and filled those slots with independent RFD4 backbones. Only c3_v_l65_E5Q survived into v7.

Approach 3 — RFD4-Proteina (11 designs from round 1, plus 1 from round 2): We used NVIDIA's RFD4-Proteina diffusion model as an orthogonal generation method, targeting the same GLP1R ECD groove with hotspot conditioning on W33, W39, R40, E68, Y69, W91, Y88, and R121. 200 designs were generated, filtered to exclude transmembrane-like sequences (max 19-residue Kyte-Doolittle window < 1.6, GRAVY < 0.3) and require sufficient charged fraction (>15%) for soluble expression. Eleven designs were selected across the round-1 pool: 2 by charge balance and hydropathy, 2 specifically selected for high Trp/Tyr content (9-12%) to provide aromatic complementarity for the W33/W39/Y69-lined groove, and 1 replacing a low-WY design (1.5% WY) with a Trp/Tyr-enriched alternative (11.5% WY, 2W+4Y). A twelfth RFD4 design came from an independent round-2 campaign described below.

All 20 designs were independently validated using ESMFold (facebook/esmfold_v1) single-sequence structure prediction, achieving refold pLDDT 80.3-93.5. Independent biophysical review by dual AI agents screened for extreme charge, tandem repeats, aggregation-prone hydrophobicity, poly-amino-acid runs, and transmembrane-like segments. Design lengths range from 52-100 residues with net charges from -10 to 0. The final portfolio has a mean Trp+Tyr fraction of 6.3%, with 4 designs above 9% WY for aromatic groove complementarity.


Round 2 — independent AF2M screening and the v8 portfolio (current best)
A second RFD4 round generated 36 new backbones against the same 111-residue GLP1R ECD construct (chain F, residues 1-111), using the same hotspot atoms and three length bins. ProteinMPNN produced two sequences per backbone; after ESMFold refold and four pre-registered soluble/non-TM filters, 24 designs were screened as 24 complexes with AlphaFold2-Multimer v3 (5 models, 3 recycles) on five A40 GPUs.

Screening was scored under gates fixed BEFORE any round-2 score existed: complex ipTM >= 0.850, refold pLDDT >= 80 with >= 90% of residues >= 70 and CA RMSD <= 3.0 A vs the designed backbone, binder pLDDT >= 80, >= 5 groove residues contacted, >= 20 contacts <= 5 A, chain integrity, and ZERO heavy-atom pairs < 2.0 A between binder and target. Of 24 screened designs, 8 satisfied every condition. Heavy-atom overlap was the dominant failure: 15 of the 24 carry at least one pair < 2.0 A, and for 11 designs it was the only listed reason. This overlaps other rejection reasons; it is not a claim that clashes explain every failure.

One candidate, B_26102611_s1 (ipTM 0.930), met every gate, was near-identical to no retained design, and added a new backbone cluster. It replaced the single conservative point-mutant c3_v_l65_E5Q in the one permitted slot. The resulting file is results/c3_submission_v8_20261025.csv: 20 designs, the other 19 byte-identical to v7 and in v7 order. Under the binding diversity unit (global connected components over all measured designs) the portfolio represents 16 independent backbone clusters versus 15 for v7; the +1 is a genuine gain, and alternative counts (direct singletons 16->18, submitted-row components 18->19) are reported separately and must not be mixed with that baseline.

Nothing here establishes receptor activation. All designs are ECD-groove binders; agonism remains unmeasured.
