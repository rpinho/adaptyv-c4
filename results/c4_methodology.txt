We combined two complementary de novo design approaches to generate conditional TfR1 binders with pH-dependent binding (strong at pH 5.5, weak at pH 7.4), exploiting His707 protonation as the switching mechanism.

Approach 1 — BindCraft (2 designs): We used BindCraft with forced targeting to design binders against TfR1 apical domain epitopes near His707 (PDB 1CX8). Designs were filtered for iPTM, pLDDT, and interface quality, then independently validated by ESMFold single-sequence refold.

Approach 2 — RFD4-Proteina (18 designs): We used NVIDIA's RFD4-Proteina diffusion model to generate binders targeting the TfR1 apical domain from PDB 11QC. Two specs were used: (a) E1 plain — no histidine bias, targeting the His707 patch (chain A residues 567-616, chain B 565-575) with hotspot conditioning on Ser583, Ser585, His586/His707, Glu593, and Leu609; (b) E1 His015 — same target with 15% histidine bias in the designed chain to engineer pH-sensitive contacts. 200 designs were generated per spec (400 total). Candidates were selected by a composite score balancing histidine content (for pH switching) and length preference (70-110 residues for expression), with diversity filtering (≥30% Hamming distance between selected designs) and exclusion of poly-His runs, cysteines, and sequences exceeding 15% histidine.

Candidate selection was iterative across two review cycles. In the first review, 17 candidates were screened by dual AI reviewers for biophysical red flags (extreme charge, tandem repeats, aggregation-prone hydrophobicity); three were dropped, and four replacements were drawn from the pool. In the second review, four more designs were dropped: two for exact internal tandem repeats (39 and 25 residues), one VHH nanobody with insufficient histidine for pH-switching (1 His in 117 residues), and one with isoelectric point at pH 7.4 causing precipitation risk at the measurement condition. These were replaced with candidates selected for strong charge swing between pH 5.5 and 7.4 (Δq > 10), mid-chain histidine clustering, and isoelectric points away from both measurement pH values.

Approach 3 — Charge-optimized variants (5 designs): Designs with extreme negative charge (−12 to −17) were improved by E→Q and D→N substitutions at positions not adjacent to histidine residues, preserving the pH-sensitive contacts while improving predicted solubility and expression. Mutations were selected at positions ≥4 residues from any His. Two strategies were used: conservative (4 mutations per design, targeting the most central positions) and maximum (all safe E/D positions). The conservative approach was preferred after review showed that aggressive optimization can introduce Q/N-rich low-complexity stretches. A third review found that one conservative variant (c4_v_33_4x) had all 4 mutations clustered within a 7-residue window adjacent to a key His pair, which would lower their pKa and shift the pH-switch midpoint; this was corrected by redistributing the mutations across positions 30-50 while preserving the same net charge and His contacts.

Candidate selection was additionally refined by isoelectric point (pI) validation. Continuous-bisection pI calculation identified two designs with pI within 0.1 pH units of the acidic measurement point (pH 5.5), implying near-zero net charge and maximal aggregation risk at that condition. These were replaced with pool candidates having pI ~5.96 and improved charge swing (Δq > 10).

All 20 final designs were validated by ESMFold (facebook/esmfold_v1) independent refold, achieving mean pLDDT scores of 77.6-95.7. Design lengths range from 73-118 residues, with histidine fractions of 8.0-15.8% across the E1 designs (median 12.2%). Net charges range from −9 to −2 (improved from −17 to 0 in the initial pool). No designs contain cysteines, no exact internal repeats ≥10 residues, and all poly-His runs are ≤3 residues.
