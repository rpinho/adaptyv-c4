Verdict

Green-light with changes — the direction is right, the order is wrong, and one non-negotiable deliverable doesn't exist

The pivot's direction is correct. But Codex treats the five-seed failure as diagnostically open when the repo's own data closes it: switching from the 73-residue crop to the full 639-residue native monomer destroyed epitope acquisition. So point 2 isn't the second move, it's the whole move, and it should go first. Point 1 is worth doing but is scoped to re-ask a question the data already answered; re-scope it to the admission gate and it costs $0 GPU. Point 3's leniency is legal under the rules but defends the wrong axis — the binding constraint is diversity, not threshold strictness. Point 4's Oct 24 is unrealistic for new designs yet far too late for the thing that genuinely doesn't exist: there is no submission-CSV builder anywhere in this repo.

The central question: generation, or miscalibrated screen?

Both — in different pools, and that distinction is the whole story. NEXT_MOVES.md collapses them.

Three things make this generation and not thresholds:

  1. The binder never reaches the epitope. interface_contacts uses a 20 Å cutoff (campaign.json). At 50–57 Å that term is saturated — identically zero gradient toward the epitope. Confirmed empirically: flat or worse at step 50 in 3 of 5 replicates.
  2. The binder isn't a protein. adjacent_CA_min_A bottoms out at 0.16–0.79 Å. Real backbone Cα–Cα is ~3.8 Å. Residues are collapsing onto each other.
  3. The loss that fell is the wrong one. Replicate 2: total 17.55 → 12.34, of which compactness 5.53 → 0.56 is nearly all of it. plddt_loss 0.0761 → 0.0754 — implied pLDDT 0.239 → 0.246 over fifty steps. The optimizer converged (grad norm 58 → 2.0) on a collapsed blob floating 56 Å off the receptor.

No threshold change rescues this. The gate is i_pTM >= 0.7; observed is 0.16–0.19. Not a near-miss.

Pool B — the old 73-residue crop pool: the screen was roughly right, the target was wrong

results/v3_all/!_Refolded.csv, 313 designs, scored against the exact gates in campaign.json:

  • 73 designs pass all eight gates. Not zero.
  • 72 more fail only i_pTM (median 0.64 vs the 0.70 gate; 85 are already ≥0.70; max 0.84).
  • Unbound_Binder_pLDDT median 0.91, max 0.97. Hotspot_Contact_Fraction median 0.8.

So the forward model sees interfaces fine and the generator can build confident folds burying 18 interface residues with zero clashes. The screen had plenty to accept.

What killed Pool B is the target. Per results/full_context_audit/RESULTS.md: "66 source candidates audited; 0 have no restored backbone clashes below 2 Å." And worse: all 73 passing designs are c4_tfr1_groove_human_forced_l* — the groove, not the apical domain. DECISIONS.md:288 records full-dimer SASA for exactly those residues: Phe685 at 0.005, Phe701 0.083, Trp702 0.126, Leu709 0.066. The groove epitope is buried in the homodimer the assay actually uses. Pool B binds a surface that does not exist on the target.

Point by point

1. Calibrate against a known binder disagree on scope

NEXT_MOVES.md wants to establish "whether our setup recognizes a real binding geometry." The repo already answered that: 85 designs at i_pTM ≥ 0.70, 73 clearing every gate, binder pLDDT to 0.97. A pipeline blind to real interfaces cannot produce that. Re-asking it with GPU is the detour.

What is unvalidated is the native-context admission gate — canonicalize() in scripts/postprocess_gpu_campaign.py:27-57, which hard-rejects anything that isn't the complete native 639-residue chain A, then applies the <2 Å clash test. That gate rejected all 66 candidates and will gate every future domain-route design. results/astra_review/ALTERNATIVES.md says it outright: "A failed known-complex restoration must be diagnosed before candidate rejection thresholds are interpreted." Codex's point 1 is a garbled restatement of Astra's sharper version.

Cheapest version — CPU only, $0 GPU, a few hours:

  1. Take PDB 6W3H (Denali TV35–TfR Fc transport vehicle), already flagged in ALTERNATIVES.md as the best verified apical control.
  2. Map its receptor entity to native 11QC by sequence/reference annotation, not author residue IDs — Astra verified the trap: that entity is a rearranged 166-residue fragment whose mappings run native 326–379 then 194–296.
  3. Superpose, transform the antibody, push it through scripts/restore_apical_context.py and then the canonicalize() + clash gates unchanged.

What falsifies the pipeline: the control being rejected. A real crystallographic complex, restored into the receptor it was solved against, must not register backbone clashes <2 Å. If it does, the gate is too strict and all 66 rejections become suspect. If it passes, the gate is sound — which is exactly the permission needed before spending GPU on point 2.

2. Domain search, then full-receptor validation agree — promote to first

Codex frames this as a search-space change. The mechanism is sharper: a 195-residue apical domain leaves AF2 nowhere to put the binder except near the epitope, so interface_contacts lands inside its 20 Å cutoff and the gradient comes alive. That is why BindCraft crops targets. The crop was retired for a real defect, but the fix for a too-small crop is a correct crop, not no crop.

Supporting evidence already in the record, uncited by Codex: V11 (forced_targeting=true) "entered the patch region but reached 1.255 Å CA overlap and malformed binder geometry" — acquisition is solvable. V12 (pLDDT weight 0.1→0.5) lifted pLDDT 0.31 → 0.51 while i_pTM stayed at 0.16 — fold confidence is tunable, the interface isn't until the geometry is fixed. Both say the bottleneck is acquisition, full stop.

TfR1-specific soundness: the apical domain is residues 189–383, a contiguous 195-residue insertion, and hotspots A205,206,208,209,210 sit inside it. Target PDB uses native numbering (chain A, 122–760) and residue 142 is GLY, correct per the challenge page's Ser142/Gly142 warning — no off-by-one. results/dimer_occlusion.json shows the apical patch is remote from the dimer interface, so monomer search is defensible even though the assay target is the homodimer.

One execution disagreement. NEXT_MOVES.md says "Complete the missing refold path on CPU before new allocation." If that means building and unit-testing the path on CPU, fine. If it means running the refold on CPU, no — a ~708-residue AF2-multimer forward pass is hours per design on CPU. Budget it as GPU. Codex should say which it means.

And the operational point neither Codex nor the study acknowledges: max_trajectories: 1, number_of_final_designs: 2. Five seeds = five trajectories. For a hard target, BindCraft's accepted-design yield per trajectory is low single-digit percent. The study was underpowered to detect success even with a perfectly healthy pipeline. NEXT_MOVES.md gets the epistemics right ("9 unsuccessful search attempts, not 9 experimentally disproven proteins") but doesn't draw the conclusion: the next campaign needs tens to low hundreds of trajectories. The five v14 runs cost $2.91 total (series.json: runtime_upper_usd) — about $0.58/trajectory. A hundred trajectories is ~$58 against $46.16 spent to date.

3. Keep promising-but-imperfect designs partial — right call, wrong axis

Codex is factually correct that no threshold is imposed. Selection weighs "method novelty, design diversity and a small set of confidence metrics, with no single metric deciding." FAQ 8: the prompt "does not rank on a single in silico metric, because most metrics do not account for the conditions we set." No pLDDT/i_pTM/PAE cutoff appears anywhere. So NEXT_MOVES.md's claim that the internal PROPKA L≤−2 is "a target rather than an official submission condition" is correct, and consistent with WIN_PLAN.md.

But Codex is relaxing the constraint that wasn't binding. The hard filters per FAQ 8 are length, nanobody/antibody annotation (ANARCI), uniqueness, and de novo zero-shot design with adequate sequence and structural diversity. Diversity is a gate, not a preference — and it's exactly where the only existing pool dies. All 73 passing designs are one epitope, one scaffold family. Three, verbatim:

73 "unique" sequences; one design. EAGEISEEEY, GPQLREM, IPDPH, QVFYRM, RDPRVF, LWEII, DEMNL — the same motif skeleton throughout. This reads as a single family to any diversity filter. scripts/novelty_audit.py exists but results/novelty_audit_selftest.json is a self-test only; no novelty/diversity report has ever been run over a real candidate set.

So: keeping imperfect designs is defensible and endorsed. Rationalizing a weak shortlist would be submitting 20 near-identical groove binders. Rank leniently on confidence metrics, strictly on diversity.

Second disagreement, on deferring pH. Mouse — yes, defer. But pH selectivity is the top-ranked objective: a measurable KD at 7.4 and no detectable binding at 6.0 ranks above everything, and each objective carries its own honourable mention. Deferring it entirely means competing only in the lowest-ranked lane (raw affinity) against ~1,500 screened designs. Don't defer — portfolio it: most of the 20 slots on plain human binders, a few reserved for pH variants of the best scaffold. The machinery exists: results/next_campaign/sensitivity.csv, and WIN_PLAN.md's best matched pair D4H/T46H on l63_59b0f47ecf134650 (HH L −1.530, HH−QQ −1.086).

4. Oct 24 / Oct 31 timeline disagree — both too late and too early

The real deadline. Submissions close Sunday November 1, 23:59 Anywhere on Earth; FAQ 6 gives AoE as UTC−12 → Nov 2, 11:59 UTC = Mon Nov 2, ~06:59 EST (US DST ends Nov 1). So ~11 days, about a day more than "Nov 1" sounds. SUBMISSION_STATUS.md has this right. Codex's Oct 31 internal deadline leaves ~1.3 days of slack — thin but defensible.

Oct 24 is unrealistic for a package of new, context-valid designs. Critical path: 6W3H control → image rebuild with domain target → adapter in front of canonicalize() → a real multi-trajectory GPU campaign → restoration → a native full-target refold path that does not exist. Not two days.

But Oct 24 is far too late for the artifact that actually doesn't exist. grep -rl molecule_class across the whole repo returns exactly one hit: SUBMISSION_STATUS.md — prose.

The one non-negotiable gap

There is no script that emits the required name,sequence,molecule_class CSV, and the submission format has never been dry-run. It is the cheapest thing in the project and it should exist today.

Also unverified and worth resolving early: SUBMISSION_STATUS.md says "The public submit route exposes no editing/replacement terms; authenticated replacement/versioning remains unverified." Independently confirmed — none of the three public pages states whether submissions can be edited or replaced. So assume the first upload is final until the authenticated portal says otherwise. That makes an early placeholder upload risky, not safe, and inverts Codex's framing of early submission as pure risk reduction. Create the Proteinbase account now and read the portal's replacement terms — that single answer determines whether to submit early or hold.

What the Codex plan is missing (table stakes)

  1. A submission CSV builder. Zero code. Needs name,sequence,molecule_class (∈ protein/nanobody/scfv/fab_kappa/fab_lambda), rows best-first, 10–250 aa, ≤20 designs for Track 3. Build it today against whatever the current best designs are, even bad ones, so the format is proven.
  2. A diversity/novelty report over a real candidate set. It's a hard filter per FAQ 8, and the only pool is one scaffold family. Point scripts/novelty_audit.py at real candidates and make diversity a ranking axis.
  3. Trajectory count. max_trajectories: 1 cannot produce 20 diverse designs. Next campaign needs 10²-scale trajectories at ~$0.58 each.
  4. Multiple epitopes. Everything so far is one groove patch (now known occluded in the dimer) plus one apical hotspot set. The apical domain is a hint, not a rule — designs elsewhere are still measured and ranked. Diversity across epitopes is free insurance.
  5. The methods write-up as a selection asset. Tracks 2/3 go to Claude, which weighs method novelty. The 29 KB METHOD.md and the honest negative record — the singular-frame bug, the full-context clash audit, the acquisition diagnosis — are genuinely unusual rigor. That's a competitive advantage if written for the selector. Right now it's written for you.
  6. Fix the series.json logging bug. All five replicates record "rejected_records": [] while worker.log plainly says rejected at screen design stage i_pTM=0.19 pLDDT=0.31 due to [pLDDT]. The aggregator drops rejections — and series.json is what the next session will read.
